Planet TapeOut: from land plots to on-chain apps

A planet of fields, waterworks, and houses connects gates, circuits, containers, BEM, and gas. Start here if you are new to TapeOut.

01 · Meet the planet

Imagine TapeOut as a planet with several “countries”: processors. They exist on blockchains such as X Layer, and each processor’s contract defines its rules. To understand a country, start with those rules.

Countries, land, and houses are metaphors. They stand for processors, circuit materials, and containers—not physical countries, property, or buildings.

02 · Land plots: NAND and LATCH

You acquire the land in small plots called gates. Each plot has one of two kinds of waterworks.

NAND is like a sluice gate.Water means 1; no water means 0. It closes and outputs 0 only when both inputs carry water. In every other case, it lets water through and outputs 1.

LATCH is like a reservoir.It releases what it stored in the previous round, then stores this round’s input for the next. This is how a circuit remembers state from one tick to the next.

The original article describes the two materials as having the same mint price. Check the relevant processor’s current rules for actual prices and parameters.

03 · The deed: turning a plan into a circuit

Combine plots into a field and apply for a “deed”: fabricate a circuit NFT. Your waterworks plan is the netlist. It specifies which gates connect, where inputs enter, and where outputs leave.

The processor contract is the land registry. It checks that the connections satisfy its netlist constraints and that feedback passes through a LATCH. Once accepted, the gates used are burned in exchange for the circuit deed. Fabrication here means creating an on-chain circuit, not manufacturing a physical silicon wafer.

The deed records its material size, NL—the total NAND and LATCH count—and an address such as 1.2.344. The netlist and circuit information are recorded on chain. Whoever holds the NFT is the circuit holder.

Running water through the field is circuit evaluation: supply inputs and observe outputs. A round is a tick; LATCH carries state to the next tick. Test vectors are like an inspection: apply specified inputs and check the expected outputs. A larger circuit does not automatically mean a better design or higher rewards.

04 · The house: containers and on-chain files

Once you have a deed, you can build a “house”: activate a container. Its address is derived from the circuit and tied to it. Holding a circuit does not require activating its container.

Moving things into the house means uploading files. A storefront can be a website; a locked box can be an encrypted file.

The walls are glass: public files can be read.Encrypt private content before uploading and manage its keys carefully. Content encrypted to a recipient’s public key needs the matching private key to decrypt it.

Updates append versions.Adding a new version does not erase the previous one from the chain. Readers select versions according to their rules. Content disappearing from a page does not mean the old data has been deleted.

The house can also have a checkout.Containers can receive assets. Paying a container and paying its current holder are different destinations; supported assets and actions depend on the contract and application.

05 · Materials, money, and road tolls

Three concepts to keep separate
In the storyIn TapeOutWhat it does
Land plotsNAND / LATCHMaterials consumed when fabricating a circuit.
MoneyBEMAn asset you can transfer or hold, also used for ecosystem rewards or application payments.
Road tollsGasNetwork fees for submitting transactions; X Layer uses OKB.

Paying a shop and paying the network are separate. For example, when transferring BEM to a container, the recipient receives BEM while the sender also pays network gas. Reading data is different from submitting a transaction.

06 · Transferring the deed and protecting your seal

You can transfer a deed by transferring the circuit NFT. Control of its associated container follows the circuit holder. Files already recorded on chain do not disappear when ownership changes.

A wallet signature is like the owner’s seal. Someone else can display a sign saying “this is mine,” but that does not replace your signature. Keep private keys safe: the protocol cannot reissue a lost private key for you.

One person can hold several circuits across different processors. Deeds need not be used only for houses: applications can use them as identities, access credentials, or transferable memberships and tickets.

07 · Find your way around the ecosystem

With these concepts in place, the products become easier to understand:
